Straddling the equator where West African tropical rainforests converge with East African golden savannahs, Uganda is celebrated worldwide as the Pearl of Africa. Across its 10 national parks and protected reserves, Uganda packs an astounding density of wildlife—sheltering more than half of the planet's remaining mountain gorillas, over 1,060 bird species, and 345 mammal species in an area comparable in size to the United Kingdom.

Bwindi Impenetrable Forest

Southwestern Uganda (Kanungu, Kisoro & Rubanda)
400+Gorillas
321Sq Km
1,160–2,607mAltitude

Blanketing the steep ridges of the Albertine Rift Valley, Bwindi Impenetrable National Park preserves one of Africa’s oldest and most biologically diverse primary rainforests, dating back more than 25,000 years. Bwindi is the premier global sanctuary for endangered mountain gorillas (Gorilla beringei beringei), protecting roughly half of the entire wild population. Trekking through its mist-draped, vine-entangled canopy to spend one profound hour with a habituated gorilla family is consistently hailed by our guests as the single greatest wildlife encounter on Earth.

Highlights & Biodiversity

  • Mountain Gorilla Tracking: Over 20 habituated gorilla families monitored by Uganda Wildlife Authority (UWA) rangers across 4 sectors: Buhoma, Ruhija, Rushaga, and Nkuringo.
  • Exceptional Primates & Mammals: Home to 120 mammal species, including rare L’Hoest’s monkeys, black-and-white colobus, baboons, and shy forest elephants.
  • Birding Mecca: 350 recorded avian species with 23 Albertine Rift endemics, including the African green broadbill and Neumann’s warbler.
  • Indigenous Batwa Culture: Learn ancient forest survival skills and cultural traditions directly from the indigenous Batwa community.
Permit: $800 UWA (FNR) Best Time: Jun–Aug & Dec–Feb Travel: 8–9h drive or 1h flight (Kihihi/Kisoro)

Uganda's Largest National Park

Murchison Falls National Park

Northwestern Uganda (Masindi, Nwoya & Buliisa)
43mWater Fall Drop
3,893Sq Km Area
76Mammal Spp

Murchison Falls National Park is Uganda’s oldest and largest conservation area, bisected by the Victoria Nile as it journeys northward toward the Mediterranean Sea. The park’s dramatic centerpiece occurs where the world’s longest river funnels through a narrow 7-meter rocky cleft in the Rift Valley escarpment before crashing 43 meters into the bubbling 'Devil’s Cauldron.' Beyond the falls, the northern sector features open Borassus palm savannah teeming with iconic African megafauna.

Highlights & Biodiversity

  • The Explosive Nile Falls: Experience the immense acoustic power and perpetual rainbows of the falls from a boat base cruise or by hiking to the cliff edge at the Top of the Falls.
  • Classic Big Savannah Game Drives: High concentrations of lions, leopards, Cape buffaloes, African elephants, and the world’s largest population of endangered Rothschild’s giraffes.
  • Victoria Nile River Safari: 3-hour river cruises showcasing gigantic pods of hippos, basks of Nile crocodiles, and elephant herds bathing along the riverbanks.
  • Delta Shoebill Excursions: Private downstream boat safaris into the Lake Albert Nile Delta to spot the prehistoric, critically endangered shoebill stork.
Wildlife: 76 mammals, 451 birds Best Time: Dec–Feb & Jun–Sep Travel: 4.5–5h drive from Kampala or flight to Pakuba

Queen Elizabeth National Park

Western Uganda (Kasese, Rubirizi & Rukungiri)
600+Bird Spp
95Mammal Spp
1,978Sq Km Area

Framed by the snow-capped Rwenzori Mountains ('Mountains of the Moon') and connected by the sparkling Kazinga Channel between Lake George and Lake Edward, Queen Elizabeth National Park is Uganda’s most ecologic-diverse park. Famous for its open acacia savannah, extinct volcanic explosion craters, and sunken underground forests, it offers travelers an unmatched combination of classic game viewing, boat cruises, and chimpanzee trekking.

Highlights & Biodiversity

  • Ishasha Tree-Climbing Lions: Witness famous lion prides lounging leisurely upon large sycamore fig and albizia branches in the southern Ishasha sector.
  • Kazinga Channel Boat Safari: Sail along the 32 km natural waterway hosting the highest concentration of hippopotamuses in Africa, alongside abundant elephants and waterbirds.
  • Kyambura Gorge Chimpanzee Tracking: Hike into the 100-meter-deep 'Valley of Apes'—a subterranean tropical rainforest hosting a habituated community of wild chimps.
  • Explosion Crater Drives: 27 km scenic route winding past dramatic crater lakes, including Lake Katwe, where local miners have harvested salt since the 16th century.
Birds: 600+ recorded species Best Time: Jan–Feb & Jun–Jul Travel: 6h drive from Kampala or 2h from Kibale/Bwindi

Africa's True Frontier Wilderness

Kidepo Valley National Park

Northeastern Uganda (Karamoja Region)
77Mammal Spp
1,442Sq Km Area
475+Bird Spp

Tucked into the rugged semi-arid corner where Uganda borders South Sudan and Kenya, Kidepo Valley National Park is Africa as it existed a century ago: vast, raw, and virtually untouched. Acclaimed by CNN Travel as one of Africa's most spectacular safari destinations, Kidepo features golden plains framed by the dramatic Mount Morungole range. Because of its remote location, you can embark on hours of game drives without encountering another safari vehicle.

Highlights & Biodiversity

  • Rare Predators & Wildlife: Uganda's only park supporting cheetahs, caracals, aardwolves, striped hyenas, bat-eared foxes, Guenther’s dik-diks, and greater kudu.
  • Massive Buffalo Herds: The Narus Valley contains permanent water points attracting colossal Cape buffalo herds numbered in the thousands, alongside lions, leopards, and Rothschild’s giraffes.
  • Kidepo Sand River & Kanangorok Hot Springs: Walk across the wide dry sand bed lined with Borassus palm forests up to natural boiling mineral springs on the South Sudan border.
  • Karamojong & Ik Cultural Immersion: Visit authentic Manyatta homesteads to learn the traditions of the pastoral Karamojong and the secluded mountain-dwelling Ik people.
Vibe: Zero crowds, pristine wilderness Best Time: Sep–Mar (dry season) Travel: 2h scheduled flight from Entebbe to Apoka Airstrip

Tropical Lake Victoria Escape

Ssese Islands

Northwestern Lake Victoria (Kalangala District)
84Islands
27,500Sq Miles Lake
TropicalBeaches

Scattered like emerald jewels across the waters of Lake Victoria—the world's second-largest freshwater body—the Ssese Islands comprise an archipelago of 84 lush, forested islands. Bugala Island, the largest and most developed, features golden sandy beaches, swaying palm trees, and peaceful beachfront lodges. For travelers finishing an energetic mountain gorilla trek or cross-country safari, the Ssese Islands provide the ideal tropical haven to unwind and reflect.

Highlights & Activities

  • Beach Relaxation & Sunsets: Unwind on uncrowded white sand beaches with warm lake breezes, evening bonfires, and unforgettable equatorial sunsets over Lake Victoria.
  • Rainforest Walks & Primates: Hike through virgin equatorial forests home to playful vervet monkeys, black-and-white colobus, sitatunga antelopes, and exotic butterflies.
  • Sport Fishing for Monster Nile Perch: Set out on private boat charters to troll for legendary Nile perch (often reaching over 50–100 kg) and freshwater tilapia.
  • Island Adventures: Explore scenic dirt roads by quad bike or bicycle, take guided canoe tours, and visit local fishing communities on surrounding islets.
Ideal For: Post-safari relaxation & honeymoons Best Time: Year-round tropical climate Access: 3.5h modern ferry from Entebbe or private speedboat

East Africa's Adventure Capital

Jinja & the Source of the Nile

Eastern Uganda (Jinja District, 80 km from Kampala)
6,650kmNile River Length
Grade 5Wild Rapids
44mBungee Jump

Perched on the northern shoreline of Lake Victoria where the Nile begins its epic 6,650-kilometer journey to the Mediterranean Sea, Jinja is internationally renowned as East Africa's adventure capital. First documented for the Western world by explorer John Hanning Speke in 1862, this historic town combines colonial charm with adrenaline-pumping outdoor sports, scenic river cruises, and vibrant local arts markets.

Highlights & Activities

  • World-Class Whitewater Rafting: Tackle the thunderous warm-water Grade 4 and Grade 5 rapids of the Victoria Nile with certified river guides—one of the top rafting runs on Earth.
  • The Historic Source of the Nile: Take a motorized wooden boat to the exact geological point where Lake Victoria spills into the Nile, marked by Speke’s monument.
  • Adrenaline Thrills: Test your nerve with a 44-meter bungee jump over the river, tandem quad biking through riverbank villages, and horseback riding across rural farmlands.
  • River Kayaking & Sunset Cruises: Enjoy peaceful stand-up paddleboarding, calm flat-water kayaking, or an evening craft beer and barbecue cruise down the Nile.
Vibe: Adrenaline, adventure, history Best Time: Year-round water conditions Travel: 2–2.5h scenic drive from Kampala / Entebbe

Practical Safari Knowledge

Frequently Asked Questions About Uganda Destinations

Clear, practical answers compiled by Kewicare's licensed safari planners and local ranger guides.

Can I combine gorilla trekking in Bwindi with wildlife safaris in Queen Elizabeth National Park?
Yes, this is Uganda's most popular safari pairing. Bwindi Impenetrable Forest and Queen Elizabeth National Park are situated just 3 to 4 hours apart by road via the scenic Ishasha sector (famous for tree-climbing lions). A typical 5 to 7-day itinerary allows you to enjoy big game drives and a Kazinga Channel boat safari before proceeding into Bwindi for gorilla trekking. See our Safari Packages for sample combined routes.
How do I travel between Uganda's national parks (road vs flight)?
Most travelers explore Uganda via comfortable 4x4 safari Land Cruisers with pop-up roofs for optimal 360-degree wildlife viewing, accompanied by an experienced local driver-guide. For remote destinations like Kidepo Valley or travelers with tighter schedules, daily domestic scheduled flights run by Aerolink Uganda and Bar Aviation connect Entebbe International Airport directly to airstrips at Pakuba (Murchison Falls), Kasese/Mweya (Queen Elizabeth), and Kihihi/Kisoro (Bwindi). Learn more about our private logistics on our How It Works page.
What is the best month to visit Uganda's safari destinations?
Uganda is a year-round destination because of its equatorial position and elevated topography (1,000m to 2,500m above sea level), maintaining comfortable temperatures between 22°C and 29°C (72°F–84°F). The optimal months for wildlife viewing and gorilla trekking are the two dry seasons: June to August and December to February, when trails are firmer and wildlife congregates around permanent water sources. However, the green seasons (March–May and September–November) offer lush landscapes, migratory birds, fewer tourists, and discounted lodge rates.
How much do Uganda Wildlife Authority (UWA) park entry permits cost?
Standard 24-hour national park entry fees for foreign non-residents are $45 for savannah parks such as Murchison Falls, Queen Elizabeth, and Kidepo Valley ($35 for foreign residents; 25,000 UGX for East African citizens). Mountain gorilla trekking permits in Bwindi Impenetrable National Park are currently $800 per person for foreign non-residents ($700 for foreign residents; 300,000 UGX for EAC citizens), which includes park entry, armed UWA rangers, and advance trackers. Read our complete guide to permits on the Gorilla Trekking page.
How many days do I need for a comprehensive Uganda safari?
For gorilla trekking alone, 3 days is the minimum duration from Entebbe or Kigali. For a well-rounded safari combining gorillas, chimpanzees, and Big 5 savannah game in Queen Elizabeth or Murchison Falls, we recommend 7 to 10 days. To complete the ultimate grand circuit including Kidepo Valley, Murchison Falls, Kibale, Queen Elizabeth, Bwindi, and Jinja, plan for 12 to 16 days.
