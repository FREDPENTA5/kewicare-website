Preparation & Timing

Planning Your Trip

Essential timelines, best seasons, trip lengths, and custom itinerary options for exploring Uganda.

How far in advance should I book?

For mountain gorilla trekking, we strongly advise booking 3 to 6 months in advance—and up to 9 to 12 months ahead for peak dry season travel in July, August, and late December. The Uganda Wildlife Authority (UWA) strictly limits gorilla permits to only 8 visitors per habituated gorilla family per day across Bwindi Impenetrable National Park and Mgahinga Gorilla National Park. Once daily permits are sold out, dates cannot be added.

For standard wildlife safaris without gorilla trekking (such as savannah game drives in Murchison Falls or Queen Elizabeth National Parks), booking 1 to 2 months in advance is usually sufficient to secure preferred safari lodges and custom 4x4 Land Cruisers.

Need to travel sooner? Kewicare frequently secures last-minute gorilla permits through our direct live portal with UWA headquarters. Learn more about our booking timeline on our How It Works page or view our safari packages.

What is the best time to visit Uganda?

Uganda is an outstanding year-round safari destination. Because the country sits directly on the equator with an average elevation of 1,100 to 2,000 meters above sea level, temperatures remain pleasantly mild year-round (typically 24°C to 29°C / 75°F to 84°F).

The Dry Seasons (June to September & December to February): These are considered the premier months for wildlife viewing and gorilla trekking. Rainforest trails in Bwindi and Kibale are drier and less slippery, and wildlife in savannah parks congregates around permanent water sources like the Kazinga Channel in Queen Elizabeth and the Victoria Nile in Murchison Falls.

The Green / Rainy Seasons (March to May & October to November): The rainy seasons bring lush emerald landscapes, dramatically clear skies, and far fewer travelers. Mountain gorillas continue to be tracked every day of the year; during wet periods, they often descend to lower elevations where tender bamboo shoots flourish, resulting in shorter hiking distances. Uganda also boasts over 1,060 recorded bird species, and these months offer world-class bird watching as Palearctic migratory species arrive. How long should my trip be?

We recommend 5 to 10 days for a well-rounded Uganda safari that balances active wilderness tracking with comfortable travel times between parks:

  • 3 to 4 Days (Gorilla Express): Ideal if you have limited time or are pairing Uganda with a Kenya or Tanzania safari. You fly into Entebbe or Kigali (Rwanda) and drive directly to Bwindi for a dedicated 1-day mountain gorilla encounter.
  • 6 to 8 Days (Classic Primates & Savannah): Our most popular duration. Combines chimpanzee tracking in Kibale Forest, classic Big Five game drives and a Kazinga Channel boat safari in Queen Elizabeth National Park, and mountain gorilla trekking in Bwindi.
  • 10 to 14 Days (The Grand Uganda Circuit): Experience the best of the Pearl of Africa without rushing. Covers Murchison Falls National Park, Kibale chimpanzees, Queen Elizabeth tree-climbing lions, Bwindi gorillas, Lake Bunyonyi relaxation, and optional extensions to remote Kidepo Valley or Jinja white-water rafting.

The Mountain Gorilla Experience

Gorilla Trekking

Official permit fees, physical requirements, age restrictions, and trekking conditions in Bwindi Forest.

How much is a gorilla trekking permit?

In Uganda, an official mountain gorilla trekking permit costs $800 per person for foreign non-residents ($700 for foreign residents with East African work permits, and UGX 300,000 for East African citizens).

Uganda vs. Rwanda Price Comparison: A gorilla permit in Rwanda's Volcanoes National Park costs $1,500 per person. Uganda provides the exact same intimate 1-hour encounter with wild mountain gorillas (Gorilla beringei beringei) for nearly half the cost ($800 vs $1,500), allowing travelers to invest savings into longer safari extensions or luxury lodges.

Gorilla Habituation Experience: Uganda also offers an exclusive 4-hour habituation permit in the southern Rushaga sector of Bwindi for $1,500 per person, allowing you to spend four full hours with a semi-habituated gorilla family alongside UWA primatologists. Kewicare purchases and collects your official permit cards directly from UWA. Read our in-depth gorilla trekking guide or explore Bwindi Impenetrable National Park.

How fit do I need to be for gorilla trekking?

A moderate level of physical fitness is recommended, but you do not need to be an extreme athlete or mountain climber. Hikes in Bwindi Impenetrable National Park and Mgahinga Gorilla National Park take place across rugged, misty afro-montane terrain between 1,160m and 2,607m (3,800 to 8,500 ft) elevation. Slopes can be steep, muddy, and tangled with vines.

Depending on where the gorilla group nested the prior evening, treks can take between 1.5 hours and 6 or 7 hours round-trip. During the 7:30 AM ranger briefing at park headquarters (Buhoma, Ruhija, Rushaga, or Nkuringo), rangers match visitors to gorilla families based on age, fitness, and walking preference.

Trailhead Porters: We strongly urge every traveler to hire a local porter ($15–$20 plus tip). Porters carry your backpack, provide a steady hand on slick descents, and hiring them directly supports local families living next to the forest. For guests with mobility challenges or elderly travelers, UWA arranges comfortable traditional sedan chairs carried by a team of 8 to 12 porters. Read complete fitness guidance on our gorilla trekking page.

What is the minimum age for gorilla trekking?

The statutory minimum age for mountain gorilla trekking in Uganda is strictly 15 years old. This law is enacted by the Uganda Wildlife Authority (UWA) and rigorously verified against your original passport during morning registration at park headquarters.

The age limit exists for two vital reasons:

  • Trekker Safety & Composure: Mountain gorillas are wild animals weighing up to 200 kg (440 lbs). Trekkers must possess the emotional maturity to remain calm, avoid sudden movements, and follow ranger commands if a silverback displays dominance.
  • Primate Health: Mountain gorillas share 98.4% of human DNA and lack immunity to common human illnesses. Children are statistically more prone to contagious viral infections (like colds, mumps, and measles) that can devastate an endangered gorilla family.

Traveling with children under 15? Kewicare organizes supervised nature walks, cultural village visits, and lodge activities with trusted staff while parents trek. Children of all ages are also warmly welcomed on savannah game drives in Murchison Falls and Queen Elizabeth. Explore family safaris on our safari packages page.

Can I trek gorillas in the rainy season?

Yes. Gorilla trekking operates every single day of the year in Uganda without exception, including during the rainy months of March to May and October to November.

Because Bwindi is a tropical afro-montane rainforest, rain showers can happen on any given day, even during the peak dry months. During rainy periods, mountain trails are muddier, but tracking success rates remain exceptionally high at over 99%.

In fact, rainy season trekking offers distinct advantages: gorillas often stay at lower elevations where tender bamboo shoots and forest fruits flourish, resulting in shorter walking distances from park headquarters. You will also experience tranquil, uncrowded trails and discounted lodge rates. With sturdy waterproof boots, gaiters, and a breathable rain jacket, rainy season trekking is deeply rewarding. Is Uganda safe for tourists?

Yes, Uganda is widely regarded as one of the safest, most stable, and most welcoming safari destinations in Africa. More than 1.5 million international travelers visit Uganda every year, and the warm hospitality of the Ugandan people is legendary.

Uganda's national parks and protected reserves are professionally managed and protected around the clock by armed Uganda Wildlife Authority (UWA) rangers and the specialized Uganda Tourism Police. During forest treks, visitors are accompanied by trained rangers equipped with satellite communication.

Our professional driver-guides are seasoned experts who know regional road networks intimately. As in any large city worldwide, basic urban safety precautions are advised in Kampala (such as safeguarding valuables and taking hotel taxis at night), but on safari, travelers experience remarkable peace and serenity. What vaccinations do I need?

Mandatory Requirement: Proof of Yellow Fever vaccination is legally required for all travelers aged 1 year and older entering Uganda. You must present your original International Certificate of Vaccination or Prophylaxis (Yellow Card) at Entebbe airport immigration. The shot must be received at least 10 days before entry.

Recommended Vaccines: The World Health Organization (WHO) and CDC advise ensuring routine vaccinations are current, including Tetanus, Hepatitis A, Hepatitis B, Typhoid, Polio, and MMR.

Malaria Prevention: Malaria is endemic throughout Uganda. We strongly recommend visiting a travel clinic or your doctor to obtain prescription malaria prophylactics (such as Malarone, Atovaquone/Proguanil, or Doxycycline). Pack an effective insect repellent with 30%+ DEET, wear long sleeves in the evenings, and sleep under provided lodge bed nets.

Gorilla Health Protection: Because mountain gorillas are highly susceptible to human respiratory viruses, travelers displaying symptoms of active colds, coughs, or flu will not be permitted to trek by UWA wardens. Wearing a surgical mask while observing gorillas is mandatory. For further questions, consult our team via our contact page.

On the Ground Experience

During Your Trip

Connectivity, currency nuances, charging electronic gear, and safari packing essentials.

Will I have phone/internet access?

Yes. Cellular coverage across Uganda has expanded rapidly. The two dominant mobile networks—MTN Uganda and Airtel—offer strong 4G LTE coverage in major urban centers, regional towns, and along primary safari corridors.

You can purchase a local prepaid SIM card upon arrival at Entebbe International Airport for $5 to $10 (passport registration required by law). Data bundles are inexpensive, typically costing $10 to $15 for 10 to 20 GB.

Most safari lodges and luxury tented camps provide complimentary Wi-Fi in their central lounge and dining areas. Note that because lodges in remote valleys rely on satellite broadband, connection speeds can vary during peak evening hours or during rainstorms. While deep on rainforest hiking trails in Bwindi or Kibale, cellular signal will drop, giving you a wonderful opportunity to unplug and immerse in nature. Explore our lodge network across our destinations.

What currency does Uganda use?

The official currency is the Ugandan Shilling (UGX), with an exchange rate typically around 3,700 to 3,850 UGX per 1 USD. However, United States Dollars (USD) are widely accepted across safari lodges, park concessions, and tour operations.

CRUCIAL NOTE ON US DOLLAR CASH: Due to strict banking regulations against counterfeiting, Ugandan banks, exchange bureaus, and safari lodges ONLY accept US Dollar banknotes printed in 2013 or newer. Bills printed in 2012 or earlier, or notes with tears, ink marks, or heavy folds, will be rejected. Ensure all USD cash you bring is crisp, clean, and printed post-2013.

Major credit cards (Visa and Mastercard) are accepted at mid-range and luxury lodges, though often subject to a 3% to 5% card processing fee. We advise exchanging $100 to $150 into Ugandan Shillings upon arrival at Entebbe airport or withdrawing from Kampala ATMs to pay for local market souvenirs, roadside snacks, and tips for trailhead porters. Review our planning advice on how it works.

Can I charge my devices at lodges?

Yes. All safari lodges on Kewicare itineraries have electricity for charging camera batteries, smartphones, tablets, and laptops.

Uganda uses the British Type G three-pin plug (220–240V, 50Hz). If traveling from North America or continental Europe, bring a standard Type G plug adapter. Most modern camera and phone chargers are dual-voltage (110–240V) and require only a pin adapter, not a voltage transformer.

In remote eco-lodges powered by solar energy, in-cottage power may be scheduled (typically available in the early morning and from late afternoon through bedtime), with central high-wattage charging stations available in the main lounge. Furthermore, all Kewicare custom 4x4 safari Land Cruisers are equipped with in-vehicle USB charging ports and 220V power inverters so you can recharge your camera equipment between game drives. Review our vehicles on our safari packages page.

What should I pack?

Packing for Uganda requires a blend of lightweight comfort for warm savannah game drives and durable outdoor gear for rainforest trekking:

  • Gorilla & Primate Trekking: Sturdy waterproof hiking boots with ankle support, thick wool socks, long lightweight moisture-wicking trousers (tucked into socks to deter safari ants), breathable long-sleeve shirts (neutral earth tones like khaki, olive, or tan; avoid bright blue and black which attract tsetse flies), gardening or leather gloves for gripping vines, gaiters, and a waterproof rain jacket or poncho.
  • Savannah Game Drives: Lightweight casual clothing, a warm fleece jacket or windbreaker for cool early-morning drives, wide-brimmed sun hat, UV-protective sunglasses, and comfortable walking shoes.
  • Wildlife Photography & Optics: 8x42 or 10x42 binoculars, camera body with telephoto zoom lens (100–400mm recommended), spare batteries (mountain chill drains batteries quickly), extra memory cards, and a high-capacity power bank.
  • Health Essentials: High-SPF sunscreen, DEET insect repellent (30%+), prescription antimalarials, personal first-aid items, and a reusable refillable water bottle.

Luggage Note: If your itinerary includes scheduled domestic bush flights, a strict 15 kg (33 lb) luggage allowance in soft-sided duffel bags applies. Find more details on our gorilla trekking guide and destinations page.
